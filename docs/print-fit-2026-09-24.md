# Battery and USB-board fit correction — 2026-09-24

The first printed base exposed two assembly defects: the battery could slide
lengthwise out of its open saddles, and the USB board could not be threaded into
its channel. Its seated envelope fitted, but the fixed front stop blocked the
motion needed to engage the receptacle in the rear wall.

## Changes

- The ballast lid extends beside the battery's right end, with 0.5 mm axial
  clearance. The 8 mm wide, 3 mm thick tongue has rounded corners, chamfered edges
  and a wider root. It remains below the BMS and the upper cable exit. The left
  housing wall supplies the other axial stop; the open base still permits upward
  battery removal.
- The fixed USB front stop is removed from the base. A removable stop on the
  screwed ballast lid takes its place. It catches the lower PCB edge rather than
  relying on the component envelope, and leaves the central wire opening free.
  The original 45-degree support gussets and rear board seat remain.
- With the head and loaded lid removed, the USB board can move 20 mm forwards
  and then 30 mm upwards, fully outside the base. Reverse this path to install
  it, then fit the lid and its stop.
- Two optional test pieces are cropped from the actual base and lid. Three
  fixture pads replace the screw-post seats outside the crop and place the test
  lid at its actual height. They are included only in the test base.
- Local reliefs at the lid's rear corners and USB slots provide room for its
  complete removal. The lower slot seam stays narrow to contain the ballast;
  the upper slot sides open on 45-degree ramps. The small recess under the USB
  seat leaves 2.7 mm of lid thickness.

## What to print

First print [the USB fit test](../stl/fumex_usb_fit.3mf) and check the real board,
receptacle and soldered wires. After a successful fit, print the revised
[base](../stl/base.stl) and [ballast lid](../stl/ball_lid.stl). Other production
parts are unchanged.

The battery extension alone is compatible with the original base, but the new
lid also includes the USB stop: the complete fix requires removing the original
fixed stop or using the revised base. Replacing only the lid does not solve the
USB insertion defect of the already printed base.

## Verification and limits

The geometric checks now separate support, positive retention and assembly
access. Battery retention is checked in nine shifted/raised poses. The USB path
has a continuous swept-volume check against the base, as well as a sampled path
with the other fitted components. A thin lower PCB-edge probe independently
checks the new stop. The original fixed USB stop fails the path check.

The [negative controls](print-fit-2026-09-24/negative-controls.json) reproduce both
defects with the original printed-base and lid meshes: the old USB stop causes
20.910724 mm³ of swept interference, and the old lid fails the battery-stop
existence check. The corrected geometry must pass these same checks.

The complete loaded-lid path removes the head, battery and two lid screws,
then lifts 11.4 mm, rolls the right edge upwards 5.9 degrees, moves 20 mm forwards
and lifts fully outside the base. It is checked in 497 poses with a minimum
0.15 mm clearance gate after the initial lift. The USB board, PWM controller and
switch stay installed. PWM removal now requires removing the loaded lid first.

These tests use measured overall hardware dimensions and simplified component
envelopes. They do not prove fit with actual solder joints and wires, printed
strength, retention force or manufacturing tolerances. The small test print
addresses the actual USB fit before another complete base is printed.
